What Lifeline is, and what it is not

Lifeline is a federal programme that helps eligible households with the cost of phone or broadband service. It is a service discount, not a device programme. That distinction is the most important point in this guide, because many adverts describe Lifeline in a way that suggests a tablet is included automatically. It is not. In some areas, a Lifeline provider may offer a device as part of its own offer, but that offer depends on the provider, the area, and current inventory. A tablet is not guaranteed, even for a household that qualifies for Lifeline. If you are considering Lifeline, start with the service benefit, check the provider terms, and treat any device as a separate, optional part of the offer that may or may not be available.

Lifeline eligibility usually follows one of two broad routes. The first is programme-based eligibility: a person, or a member of their household, participates in a qualifying assistance programme. Common examples include SNAP, Medicaid, Supplemental Security Income, Federal Public Housing Assistance, and Veterans Pension and Survivors Benefit, subject to the current programme list and state rules. The second is income-based eligibility: household income is at or below a threshold set by the programme, which is expressed as a percentage of the federal poverty guidelines and can change over time. Because thresholds and qualifying programmes can change, you should always check the current official eligibility information at the time you apply, rather than relying on an old advert or a message from another person.

A household, for Lifeline purposes, is generally a group of people who live together at the same address and share income and expenses. Lifeline is normally limited to one benefit per household, not one per person. That rule can surprise families where several adults live at the same address, or where a person has recently moved. If someone else at your address already receives Lifeline, your application may be affected. If you have moved, changed your household, or now live separately, you may need to explain the change and provide evidence. Do not apply with a different address or household description in order to avoid the one-per-household rule, because inconsistent information can lead to denial, loss of the benefit, or further verification problems.

Documents are central to a Lifeline application. You will usually need proof of identity and proof of address, and then proof of eligibility. Proof of eligibility through a programme may be a participation letter, a benefit statement, or another official document that shows current participation and matches your name. Proof through income may be a tax return, pay slips, a benefit statement, or another document that shows household income for the relevant period. Keep documents recent, legible, and consistent. If your name is spelled differently on two documents, if your address has changed, or if a document is expired, be prepared to provide an updated or corrected document. Providers and programme administrators may ask for additional evidence, and a delay does not always mean a final denial.

Apply steps, in order

First, confirm the programme you are applying for. Do not confuse Lifeline with ACP, because ACP ended on June 1, 2024, and any form, advert, or message that still asks you to apply through ACP is out of date. Second, check whether you meet a programme-based route or an income-based route, and gather the matching evidence before you start. Third, choose a provider that serves your ZIP code and ask, in writing, whether it offers a device in your area, what device, if any, is offered, and what the service plan includes. Fourth, complete the application accurately, with the same name, address, and household details that appear on your documents. Fifth, respond to verification requests promptly, and keep a record of your application date, provider, and any reference number. Sixth, if approved, check the start date, plan limits, recertification duties, and what to do if you move or change provider.

Provider availability varies by ZIP code and by inventory, and this variation can be sharp. A provider may serve one town and not the next, may offer a device in one state and not another, or may run out of stock while an application is being processed. That does not necessarily mean an application was wrong, and it does not mean another provider will have the same offer. Check each provider separately, ask about stock at the time you apply, and get the current offer confirmed before you rely on it. If a provider cannot confirm a device, do not assume that a different website or a social media post can guarantee one. A tablet is not guaranteed by Lifeline, by a programme, or by this guide.

Scam warnings for Lifeline applicants

Be especially careful with messages that promise a Lifeline tablet to everyone, that ask for payment to release a free device, or that ask for a password, a one-time code, or benefit card details. A genuine Lifeline provider will explain its service terms, will not guarantee a tablet, and will not ask you to pay to unlock a free benefit. Check the provider through an official source, do not click a link in an unexpected message without checking where it leads, and do not share documents with a person or site you have not verified. If you are asked for payment, stop, keep the message, and seek advice through an official channel. Our scams guide explains these warning signs in more detail.

After approval, keep your information up to date. Tell your provider if you move, if your household changes, or if you no longer meet the eligibility rule. Lifeline benefits usually require periodic recertification, and missing a recertification request can lead to loss of the benefit. If you change provider, follow the transfer process for your area and do not assume that a device or plan will transfer with you. If you are denied, ask for the reason, check whether it is a document or household issue that can be corrected, and correct it with accurate information rather than submitting inconsistent applications.
